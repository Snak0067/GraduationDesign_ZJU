\begin{eabstract}
As artificial intelligence evolves from perceptual to cognitive paradigms, text-attributed graphs—replete with rich semantics and intricate topological relationships—have emerged as pivotal data substrates in critical domains such as social network analysis and financial risk management. While breakthrough advancements in Large Language Models have offered novel perspectives for the synergistic semantic understanding of graph data—catalyzing the nascent paradigm of Graph Foundation Models—current architectures struggle significantly when confronted with real-world tasks characterized by high-order complexity, dynamic evolution, and rigorous logical demands. Existing models face severe limitations in lossless structural perception, cross-modal semantic alignment, and trustworthy logical reasoning, thereby failing to meet the stringent generalization and interpretability standards required in high-stakes scenarios. Consequently, developing a general-purpose graph intelligence foundation model capable of deeply integrating structural topology with textual semantics, while possessing verifiable reasoning capabilities, is not merely critical for overcoming current computational bottlenecks; it is an essential pathway toward achieving human-like cognition and decision-making on complex relational data.

Addressing these challenges, this dissertation focuses on constructing a universal Graph Foundation Model. We propose a pivotal technical framework integrating lossless structural representation, deep semantic alignment, and reinforced reasoning optimization, aiming to transcend the limitations of graph intelligence perception at the fundamental mechanism level. The primary contributions of this research are as follows:

1) Euler Path-Based Lossless Serialization Representation of Graph Structure. To address the irreversibility and loss of topological information during graph serialization, we introduce a novel lossless representation method based on Euler paths. Distinct from traditional neighborhood sampling or linearization strategies, this approach exploits Euler path traversal characteristics to establish a fully bijective mapping from graph topology to linear Token sequences. By incorporating multi-identity node encoding and re-sampling mechanisms, we achieve lossless encoding of both global topology and local connectivity patterns. This effectively resolves the issue of lost long-range dependencies, providing a complete structural perception basis for the foundation model.

2) Multi-Level Cross-Modal Alignment Algorithm for Graph Structure and Text Semantics. To bridge the heterogeneity gap between graph topological features and textual semantic features, we design a multi-level cross-modal alignment strategy. This approach transcends single-dimension limitations by creatively integrating global contrastive loss based on InfoNCE, local fine-grained matching loss at the substructure-phrase level, and generative cross-entropy loss. Through joint optimization across global, local fine-grained, and semantic generative completeness dimensions, we enforce the deep alignment of graph-text representations in the latent space, significantly enhancing the model's capacity for deep understanding and semantic association of heterogeneous data.

3) A Reinforcement Learning-Based Paradigm for Enhanced Graph Reasoning. Addressing the issues of logical hallucination and uncontrollable processes in large models within complex graph scenarios, we introduce a reinforcement learning mechanism to optimize reasoning strategies. We design a composite verifiable reward function encompassing answer correctness, topological consistency, and constraint satisfaction. Operating without the need for massive human preference annotation, we utilize cold-start and online policy optimization to guide the model in generating logically self-consistent and structurally valid Chains of Thought (CoT). This substantially improves zero-shot reasoning capabilities and interpretability in complex tasks such as multi-hop reasoning and subgraph matching.

Finally, based on the aforementioned theoretical achievements, we designed and implemented a financial anti-fraud prototype system integrating node classification and graph reasoning. This system, featuring account risk grading and interpretable fraud ring mining, not only validates the effectiveness of the proposed foundation model in authentic high-risk scenarios but also demonstrates the engineering application value of this technical framework in resolving intractable risk control problems within the industry.

\end{eabstract}
