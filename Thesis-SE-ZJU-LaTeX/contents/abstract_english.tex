\begin{eabstract}
Graph-structured data with rich semantics and complex topology has become a core substrate for social network analysis and financial risk management as AI progresses from perception toward cognition. Recent advances in large language models (LLMs) have opened new avenues for joint structural–semantic understanding of graphs, accelerating research on graph foundation models. Yet, in real-world settings where higher-order relations, temporal evolution, and strict logical constraints coexist, current approaches still struggle with faithful structural encoding, robust graph–text alignment, and trustworthy, controllable reasoning—limitations that are particularly consequential in high-stakes domains demanding both generalization and interpretability.

To address these challenges, this thesis targets a general-purpose graph foundation model and proposes a unified framework that integrates high-fidelity structural representations, deep cross-modal semantic alignment, and reinforcement learning (RL)–based reasoning optimization. The main contributions are:

1)\ {High-fidelity Eulerian-path graph serialization.}
To mitigate the loss of structural detail commonly introduced by graph-to-sequence conversion, we adopt an Eulerian-path–based serialization scheme that preserves windowed topology and local connectivity patterns with high fidelity. Unlike heuristic neighborhood sampling or naive linearization, the proposed method exploits Eulerian traversal to construct a reversible mapping from the windowed subgraph topology to a linear token sequence. Under a finite context budget, it maximizes the retention of intra-subgraph connectivity and structural semantics, providing a stronger structural prior for downstream modeling.

2)\ {Multi-level cross-modal alignment between graph structure and text semantics.}
To bridge the heterogeneity between topological signals and linguistic semantics, we develop a multi-level alignment strategy that goes beyond single-granularity matching. The objective combines a global InfoNCE contrastive loss, a local substructure–phrase matching loss, and a generative reconstruction loss. Joint optimization across global, fine-grained, and semantic-completeness levels yields tighter alignment of graph and text representations in a shared latent space, improving deep understanding and semantic association over graph–text data. On node classification benchmarks (Cora, PubMed, and OGB-Arxiv), the resulting model achieves test accuracies of 91.27\%, 94.48\%, and 77.36\%, outperforming comparable methods by 2.71, 1.30, and 3.34 percentage points, respectively.

3)\ {An RL paradigm for enhancing graph reasoning.}
To reduce non-factual reasoning and improve process controllability in complex graph scenarios, we introduce an RL-based optimization scheme with a composite, verifiable reward that accounts for answer correctness, topological consistency, and constraint satisfaction. Without relying on large-scale human preference labels, a cold-start procedure followed by online policy optimization encourages the model to produce logically coherent and structurally valid chains of thought, strengthening interpretability and reasoning ability for multi-hop inference and subgraph matching. With RL tuning, the model attains accuracies of 67.37\% and 43.88\% on the Erd\H{o}s and GraphArena reasoning benchmarks, yielding gains of 1.21 and 2.78 percentage points over the baseline.

Finally, building on these methodological advances, we implement a prototype financial anti-fraud system that integrates node classification with graph reasoning. The system supports account-level risk stratification and explainable fraud-ring discovery, validating the effectiveness of the proposed foundation model in high-risk, real-world settings and illustrating its engineering value for industrial-grade risk control.
\end{eabstract}
