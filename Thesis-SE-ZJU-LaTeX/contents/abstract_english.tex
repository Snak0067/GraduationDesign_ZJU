\begin{eabstract}
    Text-attributed graphs, characterized by rich semantics and complex topology, 
    serve as critical data substrates in evolving artificial intelligence from perception to cognition, particularly in high-stakes domains like financial risk management. While the advent of Large Language Models (LLMs) has advanced graph foundation models, current architectures remain constrained in high-fidelity structural encoding, cross-modal alignment, and trustworthy reasoning under strict logical constraints. To address these challenges, we present a unified framework for a general-purpose graph foundation model that integrates lossless structural representation, deep semantic alignment, and reinforcement learning-optimized reasoning.

    1) Euler path-based lossless structural serialization. 
    Addressing the loss of structural fidelity in graph serialization, we introduce an Euler path-based mechanism that achieves full-fidelity encoding of local topologies. Unlike traditional neighborhood sampling or linearization strategies, this method establishes a reversible mapping between graph structures and linear token sequences, maximizing connectivity preservation and semantic integrity within limited context windows.

    2) Multi-granular cross-modal alignment. 
    To bridge the heterogeneity gap between graph topology and textual semantics, we devise a multi-level alignment strategy. This approach innovatively fuses global contrastive learning (InfoNCE), fine-grained substructure-phrase matching, and generative cross-entropy objectives. By jointly optimizing across global, local, and generative dimensions, we enforce deep representation alignment in the latent space, significantly enhancing the model's comprehension of heterogeneous graph-text data.

    3) Reinforcement learning-enhanced graph reasoning. 
    Targeting hallucination and uncontrollability in complex reasoning, we propose a reinforcement learning paradigm driven by a composite verifiable reward function encompassing correctness, topological consistency, and constraint satisfaction. Through cold-start and online policy optimization without extensive human annotation, this mechanism steers the model toward generating logically self-consistent and structurally valid Chains of Thought (CoT).

    Finally, we demonstrate the framework's efficacy through a prototype financial anti-fraud system integrating account risk grading and explainable fraud ring mining, validating its superior performance and engineering value in real-world scenarios.
\end{eabstract}

\ekeywords{Graph Foundation Model, Euler Path Encoding, Cross-Modal Alignment, Reinforcement Learning, Financial Anti-Fraud}
