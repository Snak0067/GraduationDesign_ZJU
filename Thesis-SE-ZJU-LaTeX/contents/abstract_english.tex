\begin{eabstract}
As artificial intelligence advances from perceptual intelligence toward cognitive intelligence, text-attributed graphs—characterized by rich semantics and complex topological relations—have become a critical data substrate in applications such as social network analysis and financial risk management. In recent years, breakthroughs in large language models have opened new perspectives for joint semantic understanding over graph data, spurring rapid progress in graph foundation models. Nevertheless, when confronted with real-world tasks that involve high-order relations, dynamic evolution, and stringent logical constraints, existing methods still face substantial challenges in high-fidelity structural encoding, cross-modal semantic alignment, and reliable logical reasoning, falling short of the generalization and interpretability requirements in high-stakes settings.

To address these issues, this thesis aims to build a text-attributed-graph-based graph foundation model and proposes a unified framework that integrates high-fidelity structural representation, deep semantic alignment, and reinforcement-learning-based reasoning optimization. The main contributions are as follows.
1)\ {High-fidelity Eulerian-path graph serialization.}
To mitigate the loss of local connectivity patterns and structural details during graph serialization, we employ an Eulerian-path-based scheme that achieves full-fidelity encoding of the in-window topology and local connection motifs. Unlike conventional neighborhood sampling or heuristic linearization, the proposed method leverages the traversal property of Eulerian paths to construct a reversible mapping from subgraph topology to a linear token sequence. Under a limited context window, it maximizes the preservation of intra-subgraph connectivity and structural semantics, alleviating dependency capture issues within the local receptive field and providing a stronger structural basis for downstream modeling.

2) Node classification with Eulerian representations and multi-level cross-modal alignment. 
To bridge the heterogeneity gap between graph topological features and textual semantics, we design a multi-level alignment strategy that goes beyond single-granularity matching. The objective jointly optimizes a global InfoNCE-based contrastive loss, a local fine-grained substructure–phrase matching loss, and a generative reconstruction loss. Through joint optimization across global, local, and semantic completeness dimensions, the model tightly aligns graph and text representations in a shared latent space, strengthening its ability to associate and reason over heterogeneous graph–text data. On node classification benchmarks including Cora, PubMed, and OGB-Arxiv, the resulting model improves test accuracy over comparable methods by 2.71\%, 1.30\%, and 3.34\%, respectively.

3) Reinforcement-learning-enhanced graph reasoning.
To alleviate non-factual reasoning and improve controllability in complex graph scenarios, we introduce reinforcement learning to optimize the reasoning policy and design a composite, verifiable reward that incorporates answer correctness, topological consistency, and constraint satisfaction. Without requiring large-scale human preference annotations, a cold-start procedure combined with online policy optimization guides the model to generate logically coherent and structurally valid chains of thought, improving interpretability and performance on challenging tasks such as multi-hop reasoning and subgraph matching. On Erd\H{o}s and GraphArena, the reinforced model yields gains of 1.21\% and 2.78\% over the baseline.

Finally, building on the above techniques, we develop a financial anti-fraud prototype system that integrates node classification and graph reasoning. The system supports account risk stratification and explainable fraud-ring discovery, validating the effectiveness of the proposed graph foundation model in risk-critical settings and demonstrating its practical engineering value for industrial risk management.
\end{eabstract}
